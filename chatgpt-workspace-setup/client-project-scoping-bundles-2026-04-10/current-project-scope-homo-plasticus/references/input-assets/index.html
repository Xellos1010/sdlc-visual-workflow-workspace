The Human Biology

This Is Not an Environmental Story.
It Is a Medical One.

Microplastics and nanoplastics have been detected in nearly every human organ system studied. The emerging research connects plastic exposure to inflammatory responses, endocrine disruption, cardiovascular risk, and reproductive health concerns.

This is not speculation. This is published science — and the body of evidence is growing rapidly.

Water & Food

Bottled water, tap water, seafood, salt, beer, processed food packaging — all documented sources of daily microplastic ingestion.

Air & Inhalation

Indoor and outdoor air carries microplastic fibers. Your lungs filter them — partially. Residual particles accumulate in lung tissue.

Skin & Personal Care

Synthetic clothing fibers, microbeads in cosmetics, plastic-lined packaging — each a vector for daily dermal and oral exposure.

Bloodstream & Organs

Nanoplastics cross biological barriers. They have been confirmed in blood, placenta, breast milk, and brain tissue.
